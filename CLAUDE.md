# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

## Project Overview

ERGMEgo.jl is a Julia port of the R `ergm.ego` package (StatNet collection) for fitting Exponential Random Graph Models (ERGMs) to egocentrically sampled network data. It enables inference about population-level network properties from ego samples where each sampled "ego" reports their local network (alters and alter-alter ties).

## Architecture

The entire package lives in a single module file: `src/ERGMEgo.jl`. It is organized into these sections (in order):

1. **Data Structures** — `EgoNetwork{T}` (single ego observation with alters, alter ties, attributes; the inner constructor refuses a non-square or asymmetric `alter_ties`, a duplicated alter — `allunique(alters)`, the wide-to-long survey mistake that doubled the ego's degree — and a `true` on the diagonal, each naming the ego and the alter) and `EgoData{T}` (collection of ego networks with population size, sampling weights, survey design info; `_check_sampling_weights` in the inner constructor refuses a NaN/Inf/negative weight naming the ego index, id and value, and an all-zero vector — so `ego_design`, `as_egodata(…; weight_col)` and every downstream statistic never see one; a single zero weight is legal). Helper functions: `n_alters`, `alter_degree`, `ego_degree`, `n_alter_ties`, `summary_stats` (design-weighted mean AND median degree; `_weighted_median` is the 0.5 quantile of the weight-estimated distribution with the midpoint convention, deliberately not StatsBase's interpolating `median(x, Weights)`). One-line `show` methods for `EgoNetwork`, `EgoData` and `EgoERGMModel` in ERGM.jl's `ERGMModel` style (`_plural` helper).
2. **Data Preparation** — `as_egodata(ego_df, alter_df; aatie_df=...)`: ergm.ego-style frames (egos, ego-alter rows, alter-alter ties); alter IDs are preserved (never relabeled) so cross-ego overlap works; every column a keyword names is checked up front by `_require_column` (ArgumentError naming column, keyword, frame and the frame's columns); a duplicated `(ego, alter)` row and an `aatie_df` self-tie row `(ego, a, a)` are refused in the frames' own terms before the `EgoNetwork` constructor would. `ego_design` attaches ppopsize/weights.
3. **Ego-Specific Terms** — subtype `EgoTerm <: AbstractERGMTerm` (`import Networks: name, compute` — the shared statistic protocol, `ERGMEgo.name === Networks.name`, both re-exported so `compute(EgoEdges(), ed)` works with `using ERGMEgo` alone; `import ERGM: summary_stats`). `compute(term, ed)` returns the design-weighted per-capita statistic; `_ego_contribution(term, ego)` is the per-ego value (allocation-free for the four fittable terms — `EgoNodeMatch` through the `_count_matches` function barrier over the abstractly typed `Dict{Symbol,Vector}` column, with a `::Int` assertion on the result so the division is static); `_ergm_term(term)` maps to the ERGM.jl term whose sufficient statistic it estimates (EgoEdges→Edges, EgoNodeMatch→NodeMatch, EgoTriangle→Triangle with /3 for triple counting, EgoGWDegree→GWDegree, labelled `gwdeg.fixed.<decay>`; `decay >= 0`). `EgoDegree(d)` is descriptive-only (no ERGM.jl degree-count term). `ego_mixing_matrix` is a descriptive function returning (levels, matrix). An ego lacking the attribute of an `EgoNodeMatch` or of `ego_mixing_matrix` is an `ArgumentError` from `_missing_attribute_error` (attribute, ego, which side lacks it), and a carried attribute holding `missing` (ego value or any alter's) is one from `_missing_value_error` via `_check_attribute_values` (attribute, ego, how many alters; `_n_missing` is a function barrier that folds to nothing on a `Vector{String}` column, so the hot path stays allocation-free) — never a silent `0.0`/skip/TypeError; `fit_ergm_ego` therefore fails at the targets, before any MCMC or rng draw. `EgoTerm` is exported (as ERGM exports `AbstractERGMTerm`) with a docstring showing a custom subtype; `_ergm_term` is declared `public` (beside `_mcmc_controls`, documented in the API reference "Terms" page with a fittable custom-term example) because it is the hook a custom fittable term extends. The four built-in fittable terms are the whole fittable vocabulary — `ergm.ego`'s `nodefactor`/`nodecov`/`absdiff`/`gwesp`/`mm`/`degree` have no ego counterpart — and there is no `simulate(fit)`; both are stated in README "Known limitations" and the terms guide.
4. **Model and Estimation** — `EgoERGMModel{T}`, `EgoERGMResult{T}`, `fit_ergm_ego` (const aliases `ergm_ego`, `fit_ego_ergm`): design-weighted target statistics scaled to `ppopsize`, pseudo-population network with weight-proportional attribute replication, MCMC moment matching via the public `ERGM.mh_sample` chain sampler with ERGM.jl's public convergence tests (`ERGM.mcmc_convergence`: per-statistic t-ratios < `conv_threshold` and a Hotelling T² test at `hotelling_alpha`, evaluated on the sample of every iteration; **the sample that passes IS the final sample** — `converged`, `mcmc_convergence::ERGM.MCMLEConvergence`, `sim_stats` and the SEs all describe it, so they cannot disagree; a fresh sample at the returned θ is drawn only when `maxiter` is exhausted after a Newton step, and then that sample decides `converged`. Round 1 redrew a sample for the report and one fit in four printed `Converged: true` next to a Hotelling p of 0.0005. The `_nonconvergence_caveat` labels its numbers "on the final sample at the returned coefficients", so they always fail the rule), netsize adjustment `−log(popsize/ppopsize)` on the edges coefficient, and SEs `V(θ̂) = I⁻¹Σ_design I⁻¹ + I⁻¹/n_eff` — `ergm.ego`'s design + MCMC-estimation decomposition (`vcov_design`, `vcov_estimation`), with NO standalone `I⁻¹` term (the population network is not a draw from the model; the egos are the sample). Non-convergence warns, sets `converged == false`, and is printed by `show` and listed by `approximations` from the same `_nonconvergence_caveat`. The MCMC budget is the one helper `_mcmc_controls(m; n_samples, burnin, interval)` (declared `public`, documented in the API reference): `n_samples = max(400, min(3000, 20m))`, burnin/interval from `ERGM._mcmc_defaults(n_dyads)`. The `ppopsize < 5` guard names the size, its origin (keyword or the popsize/10·n_egos default with the numbers it saw) and the fix. Keywords follow the ecosystem vocabulary (`maxiter`, `n_samples`, `rng`; `max_iter` a deprecated honoured shim, `tol` deprecated and ignored). StatsAPI: `coef`, `stderror`, `vcov`, `confint`, `coeftable`, `nobs` (egos), `dof`; deliberately no `loglikelihood`/`aic`/`bic` (`objective == :moment`). Models must include `EgoEdges()`.
5. **Population Size Estimation** — `estimate_popsize` with Horvitz-Thompson and capture-recapture methods; an unknown `method` is an ArgumentError naming it and listing both valid ones.
6. **Simulation** — `simulate_ego_sample(net::Network, n_egos; ego_attrs, missing=:error, report=false, rng)` is the ecosystem's `Network → EgoData` conversion adapter: `require_observed(net, missing; context="simulate_ego_sample")` first (`supports_missing == true`, `missing_policies == (:error, :face)`); directed, two-mode (`Network` with `bipartite`, or a `BipartiteNetwork` method) and unknown/partial `ego_attrs` are refused with actionable ArgumentErrors; an ego's own loop is never an alter; `report=true` returns `(ed, ConversionReport(:Network, :EgoData))` recording `:edges` (when `n_egos < nv`), `:vertex_attrs` (each unrequested one), `:edge_attrs`, `:network_attrs`, `:loops` (loops flag) and `:missing_dyads` (under `:face`). Attribute columns are built once per attribute (`columns[attr] = [vals[v] for v in 1:n]`) and sliced per ego, so an isolate's empty `alter_attrs[attr]` has the same concrete eltype as its neighbours' (it used to be a `Vector{Any}`). The Networks.jl invariant table column `Network→EgoData` (`conversion_invariants.md`, footnotes [^7]/[^10]) still records the pre-0.2 unguarded adapter; the cross-repo update is requested (see Conventions) and the testset "Missing dyads and the conversion contract" pins every cell.
7. **Diagnostics** — `gof(result; n_sim, rng, burnin, interval, n_chains)` (a method of `Networks.gof`) runs `_gof_simulations`: pseudo-population from `rng`, `sample_networks(...; rng, n_chains)` with the `_mcmc_controls` budget (never the old literals 2000/200), ego samples from `rng`; p-values are `Networks.mc_pvalue` via `GOFStatistic`. Two statistics, mirroring `gof.ergm.ego`: `"ego summary statistics"` (mean degree — a fitted target, uninformative by construction — and mean alter ties; `GOF="model"`) and `"degree distribution"` (R's bins from `_degree_gof_bins(K, m)`: `EgoDegree(d)` for `d in 0:maxdeg-1` plus the internal tail term `_EgoDegreeAtLeast(maxdeg)` labelled `"degree ≥ maxdeg"`, `maxdeg = 2·max(K, 3)`, K the max OBSERVED ego degree — `degree(0:(maxdeg-1)) + degrange(maxdeg)`; R's no-tail branch `0:m-1` when `maxdeg ≥ m − 1`; per simulated sample; `GOF="degree"`. The tail is what makes every simulated row sum to 1 and lets an over-producing model be seen; the round-2 statistic stopped at K and lost up to 5 % of simulated egos from every row). `ego_gof` is a thin wrapper reading the first statistic out as a NamedTuple. The suite pins two-call equality, global-RNG non-consumption, and thread-count independence in a fresh `--threads=` process (CI's ubuntu/`1` cell runs with `JULIA_NUM_THREADS=4`).

## Golden fixtures (statnet ergm.ego), and the answer to issue ERGMEgo#1

Two fixtures, both loaded with Networks.jl's `load_golden` (which throws without `[provenance]`) and both regenerable with `Rscript test/fixtures/r/<name>.R > test/fixtures/<name>.toml` (ergm.ego 1.1.4, R 4.6.1):

- **`fauxmesa_ego_census.toml`** (`fauxmesa_ego_census.R`): `egor ~ edges + nodematch("Grade")` on `faux.mesa.high` under a **census** — all 205 actors are egos, unit weights, ppopsize = popsize = 205. Described below.
- **`fauxmesa_ego_weighted.toml`** (`fauxmesa_ego_weighted.R`): the **weighted sub-design** the census cannot exercise — egos 1, 4, …, 205 (69 egos, `ed[seq(1, 205, 3), ]`), case weights `Grade − 6`, ppopsize = popsize = 205 (netsize.adj = 0). Deterministic and asserted at 1e-9: the Hájek targets of `edges + nodematch("Grade") + gwdegree(0.5, fixed=TRUE) + triangle` scaled to 205 (`summary(egor ~ ..., scaleto=205)`) and their 4×4 design covariance (`attr(., "var")`), the pseudo-population's composition by grade (`[18, 42, 52, 50, 36, 7]` — largest-remainder rounding and R's `ppop.wt="round"` agree), the exact information of the dyad-independent model at R's estimate (a two-dyad-type closed form) and the design SE it implies. Monte-Carlo, with tolerances measured from both packages' seed spread: a single default fit's population-scale coefficients to the frozen R fit at 0.05 (4× the combined single-fit sd 0.012), the 3-seed mean to R's 5-seed mean at 0.03, standard errors at 0.08 (> 3× Julia's per-fit sd 0.024; R's design SE is within 0.004 of the exact sandwich here, unlike the census's 12 % DtDe offset). The test helper `fauxmesa_weighted(ids, w)` rebuilds the EgoData from the census with no draw. Five weighted fits take ≈ 12 s.

### The census fixture

The census is chosen because it makes two of the three compared quantities **deterministic**, so neither can be excused as Monte-Carlo noise.

**1. Targets.** Under a census they reduce to the observed network's own statistics (edges = 203, nodematch.Grade = 163). ERGMEgo.jl reproduces them exactly.

**2. The design variance — issue ERGMEgo#1, found by the fixture and FIXED.**

> **`_design_cov` was too small by exactly the factor (n−1)/n.**

Not approximately; exactly, in every entry. It summed `m² Σᵢ wᵢ²(hᵢ−h̄)(hᵢ−h̄)′` with `wᵢ = 1/n`, i.e. it divided the sum of squared deviations by `n`. The survey (SRS / Horvitz–Thompson) variance of a mean, which `ergm.ego` computes, divides by `n−1`. At n = 205 the standard errors came out **0.24% narrow**.

`_design_cov` now applies the Bessel correction (`Σ .*= n/(n-1)`) and matches `ergm.ego` outright; the testset asserts exact agreement (`design_std_errors = 1e-9`) and pins the correction against an independent `Statistics.cov` reference (`Σ ≈ m²·cov(H)/n`) and a hand-computed weighted three-ego case, so removing it cannot pass silently. This mattered more than 0.24% suggests: the fixture shows **the design component is 17× the estimation component**, so an ego standard error essentially *is* its design variance.

The fixture also freezes the design SEs of the *coefficients* with the **exact** information (`design_se_exact`, from the plain ERGM's `vcov` — MPLE = MLE for this dyad-independent model — sandwiching `Σ_design`; agrees to 1e-6), R's own MCMC estimate of the information (`r_DtDe`, 12 % off the exact `I⁻¹[1,1]`), and `ergm.ego`'s SE decomposition (`mle_se_design_component`, `mle_se_estimation_component`, `mle_std_errors`). The Julia fit's `vcov_design`/`stderror` are held to 0.06 per fit — R's 0.022 offset from the exact value plus 3× the Julia per-fit sd of 0.013.

(The other half of #1 — no strata, clusters, FPC, or without-replacement inclusion probabilities — a census cannot expose, because ERGMEgo.jl cannot express a design that would. That half of the issue stands.)

**3. Parameterization: `netsize.adj` must be mapped, not assumed.** `ergm.ego` splits the population edges parameter into a fixed offset `netsize.adj = −log(popsize) = −5.3230` plus a free `edges` coefficient (−0.6974); ERGMEgo.jl reports one number on the pseudo-population scale (−6.07). They are the same parameter in different clothes, and the comparable quantity is the **sum** (−6.0204), frozen as `mle_coefficients_population`. It is independently anchored by a plain (non-egocentric) `ergm` MPLE of the same model on the same network (−6.034) — which a census fit *must* reduce to, and both packages do.

**4. The defaults did not converge at realistic size — now dyad-scaled, with a real convergence test.** The old defaults were fixed constants (`n_samples=400, burnin=2000, interval=20`) that did not grow with the pseudo-population, so the chain stopped mixing as `m` rose: the 205-actor census returned `edges ≈ −21.9` against a true `−6.02`, off by a factor of three, on a network the size of the standard teaching dataset. (It did set `converged == false`, which is the one thing that stood between a user and a published wrong number.)

The MCMC controls now follow ERGM.jl's **one dyad-scaled rule** (`_mcmc_controls` → `ERGM._mcmc_defaults`: burnin `20·n_dyads`, interval `max(100, n_dyads ÷ 10)`, up to 3000 draws), and `maxiter` defaults to 80. The July interim rule (`3·n_dyads/2` / `n_dyads/70`) had a final-sample effective size of ≈ 23 on the census; the ERGM rule gives ≈ 150 at 0.3 s per sample. Convergence is `ERGM.mcmc_convergence` (t-ratios + Hotelling), not the July 1 % relative rule that stopped at the noise level — the seed-to-seed sd fell from 0.040/0.044 to 0.026/0.026 and the gap to `ergm.ego` from 0.052 to 0.010, so `mle_coefficients_population` is held at 0.05 (was 0.12). The census converges under the defaults in 5–25 iterations (≈ 3 s per fit) and lands within 0.1 of the `ergm.ego` MLE. All controls remain overridable; passing `nothing` selects the scaled default. The census egodata used by the tests is built from `load_dataset(:faux_mesa_high)` (checked against the fixture's frozen edge list), and the golden testset takes ≈ 25 s.

## Conventions

- All code resides in a single file (`src/ERGMEgo.jl`); there are no `include` statements.
- Types are parametric on vertex ID type `T` (e.g., `EgoNetwork{T}`, `EgoData{T}`).
- Each ERGM term is a struct subtyping `AbstractERGMTerm` with two required methods: `name(t)::String` and `compute(t, ed::EgoData)::Float64`.
- Weighted statistics use `ed.sampling_weights` paired with `ed.egos` via `zip`.
- Networks are assumed undirected (alter ties divided by 2 in `n_alter_ties`).
- Julia docstrings are used on all exported functions and types.
- Every random draw flows through the `rng` keyword (`_pseudo_population` seeding and `mh_sample`); never call bare `rand()`. The test suite pins that two fits from the same `rng` state are bit-identical regardless of the global RNG.
- Panel 2026-09 items 4 (missing/report on `simulate_ego_sample`), 6 (`rng`/`n_chains`/dyad-scaled budget through `_gof_simulations`), 7 (benchmark environment and allocation gates), 8/22 (docs on the bundled census), 13 (`Networks.z_pvalues`), 15 (StatsAPI surface), 16 (`maxiter` vocabulary), 19 (parametric result types) and 24 (`ERGM.mcmc_convergence` in moment matching) are done; the invariant-table column in Networks.jl, the capability generator's missing-policy row and the migration page's StatsAPI row are the cross-repo pieces. The open half of ERGMEgo#1 (richer survey designs) is a documented limitation (README "Known limitations", `show`, `approximations`), not a bug.
- Actionable errors are the rule: a routine that meets a mistake (missing attribute or `missing` value, unknown column, directed/two-mode/masked input, a pseudo-population below 5, an empty `EgoData` in `summary_stats`, a NaN/Inf/negative/all-zero sampling weight, a duplicated alter or an alter self-tie, an unknown `estimate_popsize` method) throws an `ArgumentError` naming the thing and the fix; it never returns a number computed around it.
- Public surface beyond the exports: `public _mcmc_controls, _ergm_term` (both documented with runnable examples — `names(ERGMEgo)` lists public names, and the docstring testset walks it); every `ERGM._name` reach-in of the source (`_mcmc_defaults`, imported by name) targets a binding ERGM declares `public`, pinned by a source scan in the "Shared contracts" testset together with `Base.ispublic(ERGM, :mcmc_convergence)`.
- Requires Julia >= 1.12.
